Let us picture the father standing at the end of the road. Every evening he walks out past the olive trees, past the stone wall his grandfather built, and he shades his eyes against the setting sun and he looks toward the far country. The neighbours think he has lost his mind. The servants whisper about it in the kitchen. His older son is out in the field working twice as hard and saying nothing, but you can see it in the set of his jaw. And still the father goes out every single evening. Now here is the part that should stop us in our tracks. When the younger son finally comes home, filthy and hungry and rehearsing his little speech about being made a hired servant, the father does not wait for the speech. He runs. In that culture a grown man of standing did not run. He would have had to gather up his robes and show his legs in the street, and the whole village would have laughed. But he runs anyway, and he throws his arms around that boy before the boy can finish a single sentence. That is the heart of God toward you. He is not standing at the gate with a clipboard, checking whether your apology is sincere enough. He is already running down the road.

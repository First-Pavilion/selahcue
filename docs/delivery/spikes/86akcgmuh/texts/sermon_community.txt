The early church in the book of Acts was not a collection of perfect people. They argued about food and about who was being overlooked in the daily distribution. They had members who lied about the price of a field. They had leaders who disagreed so sharply that they went their separate ways. And yet the scripture tells us they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer. Devoted. That is a strong word. It means they kept showing up, even when showing up was inconvenient, even when the person across the table had said something hurtful the week before. I think we have lost some of that devotion. We treat church like a restaurant, where we come for the service and leave if the menu changes. But the body of Christ is more like a family dinner. You do not get to choose who sits beside you. Your uncle tells the same story he told last year. Somebody spills the gravy. And still you come back, because these are your people, and because something happens around that table that cannot happen anywhere else. So this week, reach out to someone in this room you do not usually talk to. Ask them how they are really doing, and then wait long enough to hear the answer.

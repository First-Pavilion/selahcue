Church, I want you to turn with me this morning to the question that sits underneath every other question we bring into this room. Not what did I do this week, and not what did I fail to do, but what has God already done before I ever lifted a finger. Because grace does not wait for you to clean yourself up. Grace walks into the mess of your kitchen on a Tuesday night, when the dishes are piled high and the bills are unpaid and the children are shouting down the hallway, and grace says, I am not afraid of this. The apostle tells us that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Still sinners. Not almost finished, not nearly respectable, not halfway up the mountain. Still in the valley, still tangled in the thorns, still looking the other way. And somebody here needs to hear that the love of the Father was never a reward for good behaviour. It was the starting point. It was the foundation poured before the first brick was laid. So when the voice in your head tells you that you have to earn your seat at this table, I want you to answer it with the gospel. Tell it that the table was set long before you arrived, that your name was written on the card in ink that does not fade, and that the host himself has pulled out your chair.

There is a difference between optimism and hope, and the difference matters more than we usually admit. Optimism looks at the weather forecast and says it will probably clear up by the weekend. Hope stands in the middle of the storm and says, even if it never clears, I know who holds the sky. Optimism depends on circumstances turning out well. Hope depends on a person who has already proven himself trustworthy. Abraham was an old man when God promised him a son, and the scripture says that against all hope, in hope he believed. Against all hope, in hope. He looked at the facts, his own body as good as dead and his wife long past the age of children, and he did not pretend the facts were different. He simply decided that the promise was bigger than the facts. I wonder what facts you are staring at this morning. A bank balance that will not stretch. A relationship that seems beyond repair. A prayer you have prayed so many times that you have stopped expecting an answer. I am not asking you to ignore those facts. I am asking you to put them next to the promise and see which one is taller. Because the God who raised Jesus from the dead has not run out of resurrection power, and he has not forgotten your name.

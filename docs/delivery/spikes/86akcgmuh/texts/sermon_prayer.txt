Some of you have told me that you do not know how to pray. You say that when you close your eyes your mind wanders to the grocery list, or to the argument you had in the car on the way here, or to the email you forgot to send on Friday afternoon. And you think that means you are doing it wrong. Let me tell you something that took me far too long to learn. Prayer is not a performance. It is a conversation with someone who already knows what you are going to say and wants to hear you say it anyway. The disciples did not ask Jesus to teach them how to preach. They did not ask him how to heal the sick or how to feed a crowd of thousands. They asked him, Lord, teach us to pray. And what he gave them was simple enough for a child to remember. Our Father in heaven, may your name be kept holy. Start there. Start with who he is before you get to what you need. Then bring him your daily bread, the ordinary things, the rent and the car repair and the friend in hospital. Then bring him the things you are ashamed of, and let him forgive you, and let that forgiveness spill over onto the people who have hurt you. That is the whole shape of it.

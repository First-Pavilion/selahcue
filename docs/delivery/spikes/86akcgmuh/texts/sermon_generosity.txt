We talk a great deal in the church about giving, and sometimes I think we talk about it in the wrong key. We make it sound like a tax, like something the pastor needs from you so the lights stay on and the roof stops leaking. But generosity in the kingdom of God is not a transaction. It is a reflection. When God so loved the world, he gave, and he did not give leftovers. He gave his only son, held nothing back, kept nothing in reserve for himself. And when we give, whether it is money or time or patience with the neighbour who always parks across our driveway, we are holding up a small mirror to that first and greatest gift. I remember a widow in my first congregation who lived on almost nothing. She would come in every Sunday with a small envelope, and one day I tried to tell her gently that she did not need to give so much. She looked at me as if I had said something very foolish, and she told me, pastor, you do not understand, this is the only part of my week where I get to be like him. I have never forgotten that. The only part of my week where I get to be like him. So this is not about the offering plate. This is about becoming the kind of people who look like their father.

## Why keep the deadlines

Retaining 58 cases and moving the baseline cap from 180 s to 195 s would leave 10 s overhead before the existing 5-second cleanup reserve if all four mutants consumed their 60 s ceilings. At 200 s that margin is 5 s, of which 2.984 s was fixture execution alone in this run. At 205 s there is zero nominal overhead margin. The shared working deadline clamps downstream stages, so a longer baseline can shorten later mutant time even while their configured caps stay at 60 s. One candidate cap might cover the receipt migration on a particular runner, but the archive provides neither its complete elapsed cost nor the later teardown/reporting time or mutant costs. It provides no same-source normal full API/G1 job completion timing. A single-digit tail estimate or assumed unused mutant time cannot justify raising the cap. The seven-case choice materially reduces measured duplicate work and keeps headroom for the previously unreachable stages. It remains conditional on actual hosted evidence within current budgets.
